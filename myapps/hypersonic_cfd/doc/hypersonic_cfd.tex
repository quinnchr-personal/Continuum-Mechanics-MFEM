\section{Overview and scope}

\code{hycfd} is an implicit hybridizable discontinuous Galerkin (HDG)
solver for the steady two-dimensional compressible Navier--Stokes
equations, aimed at hypersonic perfect-gas flows and structured so that
a two-temperature thermochemical-nonequilibrium model can be added
behind the same interfaces. It was seeded from the verified
\code{myapps/hdg\_navierstokes} Exasim replica, which remains
\emph{frozen} as this code's permanent regression oracle: every test
run re-verifies that \code{hycfd} reproduces the frozen app's Mach~8.03
half-cylinder wall data.

Capabilities: runtime polynomial order $p \ge 1$; triangle, quadrilateral,
and mixed meshes; MFEM/Gmsh mesh-file input (curved elements supported);
a boundary-condition catalog of seven types; dilatation-sensor shock
capturing refreshed every Newton iteration; generic staged continuation
(Mach/Reynolds homotopy and artificial-viscosity handovers through one
mechanism); and MPI parallelism with a direct condensed-trace solve.
Out of scope: three dimensions (assembly is dimension-parameterized but
untested in 3D), time accuracy (pseudo-transient continuation only),
and ablation/ALE coupling.

\section{Governing equations and code units}

The solver advances the conservative state $u = (\rho, \rho u_x,
\rho u_y, \rho E)$ with the first-order system
\begin{align}
  q - \nabla u &= 0, \\
  \nabla \cdot \bigl(F^{\mathrm{inv}}(u) - F^{\mathrm{vis}}(u, q)
      - \varepsilon\, q\bigr) &= 0,
\end{align}
where $\varepsilon(x)$ is the artificial viscosity (AV), applied as a
Laplacian on \emph{all} components. Note the convention $q = +\nabla u$
(Exasim uses $q = -\nabla u$; the sign map is confined to the generated
kernels inside \code{physics/perfect\_gas.hpp}).

Nondimensionalization (all derived at full precision from the typed
\code{PerfectGasParams}, never from rounded deck literals):
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\rho_\infty = 1$, $|u_\infty| = 1$; freestream pressure
        $p_\infty = 1/(\gamma M^2)$.
  \item Temperature is carried in internal-energy units:
        $T_{\mathrm{code}} = e = p /((\gamma-1)\rho)$, with freestream
        value $T_\infty^{\mathrm{nd}} = 1/(\gamma(\gamma-1)M^2)$
        (\code{TinfND()}). The physical temperature enters only through
        Sutherland's law via $T/T_\infty = \gamma M^2 p/\rho$ and the
        dimensional reference \code{T\_inf\_K}.
  \item The code viscosity includes the Reynolds division:
        $\mu_{\mathrm{code}} = \mu_r(T)/\mathrm{Re}$ with the Sutherland
        ratio $\mu_r(T) = (T/T_\infty)^{3/2}
        (T_\infty + S)/(T + S)$, $S = 110.4\,$K.
  \item Conductive heat flux $ = f_c \nabla T_{\mathrm{code}}$ with
        $f_c = \gamma \mu_{\mathrm{code}} / \mathrm{Pr}$.
\end{itemize}
Useful wall relations in these units (derived and verified against the
flat-plate validation case, Section~\ref{sec:validation}): for a
boundary layer with freestream edge conditions,
\begin{equation}
  c_f \sqrt{\mathrm{Re}_x} = \sqrt{2}\, C_w f''(0), \qquad
  q_w \sqrt{\mathrm{Re}_x} =
     \frac{\gamma\, T_\infty^{\mathrm{nd}}\, C_w\, \theta'(0)}
          {\mathrm{Pr}\,\sqrt{2}},
\end{equation}
where $C_w = \rho_w \mu_w / (\rho_e \mu_e) = \mu_r(\theta_w)/\theta_w$
and $f''(0), \theta'(0)$ are the Lees--Dorodnitsyn similarity slopes.

\section{HDG discretization}

Volume unknowns $(u, q)$ live in per-element $L^2$ spaces of order $p$;
the trace unknown $\uhat$ lives on mesh faces
(\code{DG\_Interface\_FECollection}). The numerical flux is
\begin{equation}
  \widehat F \cdot n = F(\uhat, q)\cdot n + \tau\,(u - \uhat),
\end{equation}
with a constant stabilization $\tau$ (deck key \code{physics.tau};
$\tau = 1$ is the correct scale here because $|u|+c \le 1 + 1/M < 2$ in
code units at any Mach number). Element assembly proceeds as: exact
per-element gradient elimination $q = C u - E \uhat$, elimination of $u$
by a dense block solve, and static condensation onto $\uhat$. Mixed
meshes are handled by per-geometry reference tables and per-element
state offsets; quadrature order is $2p + {}$\code{quadrature\_increment}
(default increment 1).

In parallel, elements are owned uniquely; the condensed local
\code{SparseMatrix} is stitched across ranks by
$P^T A P$ (\code{MakeSquareBlockDiag} + \code{ConvertFrom} +
\code{MakePtAP}), which sums shared-face contributions. The condensed
system is solved with MUMPS through PETSc
(\code{input/petsc.opts}). Exasim-ordered reference data (\code{udg.bin},
\code{vdg.bin}) is mapped through explicit \code{serial\_element\_ids}
because \code{ParMesh} renumbers elements.

\section{Physics interface and boundary conditions}

All physics flows through the virtual \code{PhysicsModel} interface
(runtime component count, flux with analytic Jacobians or an FD
fallback, admissibility, wave speeds, named outputs) with
\code{PerfectGasModel} as the sole current implementation. Boundary
conditions are trace-row residuals $f_b(\,u, q, \uhat, n, x\,) = 0$
registered per model:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
\code{bc:} type & Behavior \\
\midrule
\code{freestream}            & full-state Dirichlet at $u_\infty$ \\
\code{supersonic\_outflow}   & full extrapolation $u - \uhat$ \\
\code{isothermal\_wall}      & no slip; energy pinned to
                               $T_{\mathrm{wall}}$ (\code{Twall\_K}) \\
\code{adiabatic\_wall}       & no slip; zero \emph{numerical} energy
                               flux $F_E\cdot n + \tau(u_E-\uhat_E)=0$ \\
\code{slip\_wall}            & $n\cdot\uhat_m = 0$, tangential
                               extrapolation, adiabatic energy row \\
\code{characteristic\_farfield} & flux splitting
                               $A_n^+(u-\uhat) + A_n^-(u_\infty-\uhat)$,
                               frozen at freestream \\
\code{pressure\_outlet}      & mass/momentum extrapolated; energy row
                               targets $E(p_{\mathrm{out}})$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Two hard-won facts. First, the adiabatic condition must be imposed on
the \emph{numerical} flux: constraining only the conductive flux leaves
$\uhat_E$ without a well-posed trace equation. Second, at high Reynolds
number the adiabatic (and slip) wall energy level is a near-neutral
mode with only an $O(1/\mathrm{Re})$ conduction restoring force; cold
starts against such walls are near-singular for steady Newton (see
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\section{Nonlinear and linear solvers}

\code{DampedNewtonSolve} (\code{solvers/newton.hpp}) implements damped
Newton with two acceptance regimes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Pure Newton}: Armijo backtracking,
        $|R(x+\alpha d)| \le (1 - c_1 \alpha)|R(x)|$, halving from
        $\alpha = 1$ to \code{alpha\_min}; failure aborts the solve.
  \item \textbf{PTC} (pseudo-transient continuation): backward-Euler
        volume mass term $M/\Delta\tau$, switched-evolution-relaxation
        step growth, and \emph{plain-decrease} acceptance --- the
        exactly-solved damped step reduces the residual by only
        $O(\Delta\tau)$, so the Armijo test would reject
        systematically. Rejection at every $\alpha$ shrinks
        $\Delta\tau$. PTC turns off below
        \code{newton.ptc\_off\_residual}.
\end{itemize}
Optional guard \code{require\_admissible}: line-search trials with
non-positive minimum density or pressure are rejected exactly like
non-finite residuals. With the smoothed $\rho/p$ floors active
(\code{regularization: floors}), a globally decreasing step can
otherwise park the state in floor-land, where the Jacobian is
degenerate and Newton stalls.

The \code{NewtonPrepare} hook is called at the top of every iteration,
before assembly, with the previous iteration's residual; whatever it
installs stays frozen through that iteration's Jacobian and line
search. It exists to support adaptive frozen-per-iteration artificial
viscosity (next section) but is generic.

\section{Shock capturing}
\label{sec:av}

The dilatation sensor (\code{discretization/av\_sensor}) builds an
elementwise amplitude
\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon_K = \lambda\, h_K \max_{\mathrm{qp}}
     \Bigl[ (|u| + c)\; s\Bigl(
        \frac{-\nabla\!\cdot\!u\; h_K}{(p+1)\,\kappa\, c}\Bigr) \Bigr],
\end{equation}
with a compact-support smoothstep ramp $s$ (identically zero below
$0.2$, one above $0.8$) --- resolved smooth flow produces \emph{exactly}
zero AV --- followed by vertex-max smoothing onto a continuous $H^1$
order-1 field (parallel via \code{GroupCommunicator} max reduction).

\code{SensorAVController} realizes \emph{adaptive frozen-per-iteration
AV}: at the top of every Newton iteration the sensor is re-evaluated
from the current iterate, under-relaxed by an exponential moving
average (\code{av.relax}), floored by a bootstrap profile that decays
linearly over \code{av.bootstrap.decay\_iters} iterations (cold starts
need AV where the shock \emph{will} form), and installed as frozen
tabulated data. Once the residual drops below
\code{av.freeze\_residual} (after the bootstrap window) the field
freezes permanently and Newton closes quadratically. The per-solve
lagged fixed point this replaced limit-cycled: the shock drifted
between solves faster than outer under-relaxation could track. At
Mach~8 the sensor--shock fixed point hovers chaotically around
$10^{-3}$ without the freeze; with it, the campaign closes in one
iteration from the freeze point. Setting
\code{freeze\_residual} $=\infty$ gives install-once frozen-sensor
semantics (evaluate once from the initial state, never refresh).

Two interaction rules learned the hard way: frozen AV must never
overlap a damped-initialization layer (it wrecks the first Newton
directions), and switching AV profiles between warm-started stages is
itself a shock to the solve --- prefer install-once sensors or
same-profile stages.

\section{Continuation}

\code{continuation.stages} is a list of YAML \emph{override maps}
applied on top of the base deck (not cumulatively). Allowed keys:
\code{physics}, \code{av}, \code{newton}, \code{ptc}. Maps merge
recursively, except that a map whose \code{mode} differs replaces the
block wholesale (so stale sibling keys cannot leak across AV modes).
The physics model, operator, and output writer are rebuilt per stage;
the state carries over with the trace re-initialized from the interior.
Mach/Reynolds homotopy, AV ramp-downs, and sensor-to-tanh handovers are
all expressed through this one mechanism. The flagship campaign
(\code{input/mach8\_sensor.yaml}) runs a sensor stage from a damped
freestream followed by three tanh ramp-down stages onto the frozen
reference AV profile, and reproduces the frozen app's wall data to
$1.2\times10^{-5}$ (pressure coefficient) and $2.4\times10^{-5}$ (heat
flux).

\section{Verification and validation}
\label{sec:validation}

\code{make check} runs the fast gates: generated-kernel parity against
the Exasim oracle headers ($10^{-16}$), FD Jacobian checks for every
flux and boundary condition, manufactured-solution convergence at
$p = 1..4$ on quadrilateral/triangle/mixed/curved meshes, the PTC
limit fixture, and the sensor-AV unit gates. \code{make test} adds the
$np = \{2,4\}$ parallel consistency fixtures, the frozen Mach~8
regression (serial and $np=4$; wall $C_p$ within $8.8\times10^{-6}$,
$q_w$ within $2.1\times10^{-5}$ of the frozen oracle), the M4.5
sanity and sensor decks, the Mach~8 sensor campaign, and the validation
suite below, serial and at $np=4$.

Notes on the reference choices. The bow-shock standoff is gated against
Sinclair \& Cui, \emph{Phys.\ Fluids} \textbf{29}, 026102 (2017),
Eq.~(32), not Billig's correlation: Billig's cylinder fit underpredicts
standoff for $M \lesssim 6$ (their Fig.~7, against Euler solutions and
the Alperin/Kim/Kaattari experiments), which this suite reproduces ---
the computed standoffs sit $4$--$7\%$ above Sinclair--Cui and
$18\%$/$9\%$ above Billig. The standoff itself is measured by an
outer-in density mid-jump crossing on the stagnation line
(\code{StagnationDensityCrossing}); the older maximum-gradient
diagnostic locks onto the near-wall thermal layer at high Mach and is
retained only as a regression fingerprint. The stagnation $C_p$ runs
$2$--$3\%$ below the inviscid pitot value because the AV-thickened
shock produces slightly more entropy than a discontinuity.

The flat plate solves a Reynolds continuation ladder
$\{10^5, 3\times10^5, 10^6\}$ at $M = 4$ (cold isothermal wall,
pure Newton, admissibility guard, damped start at the resolved
boundary-layer scale, plate beginning at the inflow plane) and compares
wall $c_f$ and $q_w$ pointwise against a compressible-Blasius shooting
solution (Sutherland $C(\theta)$, RK4 with two-parameter Newton
shooting and Mach continuation; the solver self-checks the
incompressible limit $c_f\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}_x} = 0.664$). The oblique
shock case prescribes the exact Rankine--Hugoniot piecewise weak
solution on all boundaries through the Dirichlet override, seeds the
interior with its projection, and verifies that the captured, sensor-
regularized shock stays put and reproduces the downstream state.

\section{Known limitations and practical guidance}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Slip-wall cold starts.} A state with $O(1)$ residual
        concentrated in slip-wall trace rows (e.g.\ an impulsive
        freestream over a ramp) produces pathologically scaled Newton
        corrections ($|\delta| \sim 10^2$ for a residual of $10^{-2}$,
        descent only at $\alpha \sim 10^{-3}$), independent of the
        initialization, Reynolds number, AV, and PTC. PTC cannot help:
        it damps only the volume block, and the condensed system
        degenerates toward the bare trace block as
        $\Delta\tau \to 0$ (it can reach exact MUMPS singularity).
        This is why the compression-corner validation is formulated as
        a Dirichlet-driven oblique shock. Candidate fixes: trace-block
        pseudo-time damping, or a rescaled slip-row formulation.
  \item \textbf{PTC on strongly anisotropic meshes} inherits the same
        $\Delta\tau \to 0$ degeneracy; the flat-plate case therefore
        runs pure Newton with the admissibility guard.
  \item \textbf{Leading-edge singularities} (sharp flat plate) develop
        a spurious expansion spike that deepens with Reynolds number;
        at $M = 6$ this defeats Newton beyond
        $\mathrm{Re} \approx 10^5$ on the current meshes, and a frozen
        AV patch cannot be switched on mid-ladder without shocking the
        warm start. The validation case runs at $M = 4$, where the
        spike stays benign through $\mathrm{Re} = 10^6$.
  \item \textbf{Sensor-AV runs are chaotic run-to-run} (element on/off
        flicker amplifies solver-level floating-point noise). Gates
        must not depend on exact transient trajectories; the residual
        freeze and tanh handover make campaign endpoints deterministic.
  \item \textbf{Pure-sensor solutions cannot match the frozen oracle's
        wall heat flux}: the Exasim reference AV is the global
        $0.025\tanh(5(r-1))$ profile, which dominates the physical
        viscosity through most of the boundary layer. Campaigns gated
        against the oracle must hand over to that profile first.
\end{itemize}
