\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This manual documents how the flux-kernel framework of the root \file{src/}
library is verified.  It describes the application executable and the
scripts under \file{apps/}, the example meshes, and every input under
\file{apps/input/}: the problem it poses, the reference it is compared
with, the quantity checked and the tolerance, and the test that runs it.
The unit-level checks of the test suite (\file{tests/}) are summarised
first, since they verify the ingredients the application cases rely on.
The models and methods themselves are described in the theory manual
(\file{doc/theory\_manual.tex}); the closed-form solutions of the
homogeneous states of the incompressible models are collected in
Appendix~\ref{app:homogeneous}.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Levels of verification}

The checks form three tiers, each relying on the previous one.

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Unit and consistency tests} (\file{tests/test\_base},
    \file{test\_materials}): tensor algebra against hand values, dual
    derivatives against finite differences, the YAML schema and the
    expression parser, and every material's stress-free reference state,
    tangent, objectivity and small-strain limit.
  \item \textbf{Discretisation tests} (\file{tests/test\_solid\_mms},
    \file{test\_mixed}, \file{test\_homogeneous}, \file{test\_loading},
    \file{test\_linear\_elasticity}, \file{test\_dynamics},
    \file{test\_viscoelastic}, \file{test\_axisymmetric},
    \file{test\_thermoelastic}, \file{test\_scalar\_transport}):
    patch tests, assembled Jacobians against finite differences, manufactured
    solutions with measured convergence rates, homogeneous states against
    closed forms, the boundary-condition and load-scheduling features, the
    time integration, the quadrature-point history of a rate-dependent
    material with the rigid-sphere contact, the axisymmetric kinematics
    against the plane-strain and three-dimensional closed forms, the
    coupled thermo-mechanical problem against the material point, the
    series solution of conduction and finite differences, and the scalar
    transport kernel and module against the stock integrators, finite
    differences, manufactured solutions and a series solution.
  \item \textbf{Application cases} (\file{apps/input/}, driven by
    \file{tests/test\_verification}, \file{test\_linear\_verification},
    \file{test\_benchmarks}, \file{test\_loading}, \file{test\_parallel},
    \file{test\_scalar\_verification} and the comparison scripts of
    \file{apps/}): complete inputs run through the same library wiring as
    the executables and compared with analytical solutions, literature
    benchmarks, frozen regression values, a reference code, or the drivers
    of \file{myapps/}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The executables}

\file{apps/solid\_mechanics.cpp} contains no physics: it parses the input,
builds the parallel mesh, creates the problem selected by
\code{formulation}, installs the loads, and runs the quasi-static stepper
with a callback that writes ParaView cycles, probes and reactions after
every converged increment.  With a \code{dynamics} block in the input it
wraps the same problem with inertia and runs the same loop in physical time;
the header then lists the scheme, the time steps and the time dependence of
every load and Dirichlet entry, the per-step lines gain the energies
(\code{output.energy}), and the reactions include the inertia a support
carries.  At startup it prints the mesh statistics and
the element and boundary attributes with their names; at the end the
Newton report, $\|\bu\|_{L^2}$, the internal energy, the probes and the
reactions.  The exit status is 0 on convergence, 2 if the load path did
not complete, and 1 on an input error.  Run as
\begingroup\small
\begin{verbatim}
./build/apps/solid_mechanics -i apps/input/finite_elasticity/cooks_membrane/cook.yaml
mpirun -np 4 ./build/apps/solid_mechanics -i <input.yaml>
\end{verbatim}
\endgroup
\file{apps/scalar\_transport.cpp} is the same wiring for the scalar
transport physics (theory manual, Section~``Scalar transport''): it parses
that physics' schema (a top-level \code{physics: scalar\_transport} key
marks an input as its own; either executable rejects the other's input
with a message naming the right one), builds the problem with its laws,
conditions and exact solution, and runs the stepper in the pseudo-time or
in physical time with a callback that writes the ParaView cycles, the
Newton count, the errors against \code{output.exact} (also to
\file{error\_history.csv} next to the collection), the flows through the
Dirichlet entries (\code{output.flows}, \file{flows.csv}) and the probes
after every accepted step; at the end the $L^2$ norm of the unknown, the
errors, the probes and the flows.  Run as
\begingroup\small
\begin{verbatim}
./build/apps/scalar_transport -i apps/input/scalar_transport/transient_diffusion_mms.yaml
\end{verbatim}
\endgroup

\section{Unit and consistency tests}

\subsection{\texttt{test\_base}}
Tensor algebra (determinant, inverse, contractions of rank 2 and 4) against
hand values in two and three dimensions; dual-number derivatives of scalar
composites and of $\det$, $\mathrm{inv}$ and tensor compositions against
Richardson-extrapolated central differences to $10^{-10}$; the YAML
schema: the documented example parses to the expected values, defaults for
omitted sections, malformed files give an error rather than a crash,
missing keys, wrong types, unknown keys and bad enumerations name the full
key path; boundary attributes by number and by physical-group name; load
schedules and their YAML forms (ramp, constant, table, components, step
segments, bisection control); the expression grammar (precedence,
right-associative power, functions, comparisons, parse errors with column
numbers); and material regions (inheritance, bulk-key replacement, errors).

\subsection{\texttt{test\_materials}}
For every model, at five random deformation gradients $\bF = \bI + 0.4\bR$
with $\det\bF > 0.3$:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item stress-free reference state $\bP(\bI) = \bm 0$ to machine precision;
  \item the dual-number tangent against central finite differences of
    $\bP$ (step $10^{-6}$), relative error $\le 10^{-6}$;
  \item objectivity $\bP(\bm Q\bF) = \bm Q\bP(\bF)$ for random rotations
    $\bm Q$ to $10^{-12}$;
  \item the small-strain limit: for $\bF = \bI + \epsilon\bm H$ the stress
    equals $\lambda\tr\bm\varepsilon\,\bI + 2\mu\bm\varepsilon$ with the
    resolved moduli ($\lambda = \kappa - \tfrac23\mu$ for the decoupled
    models);
  \item for the decoupled models, $\bP = \partial\Psi/\partial\bF$ and
    $\bP_{\mathrm{iso}} = \partial\Psi_{\mathrm{iso}}/\partial\bF$ by
    duals against finite differences, and the deviatoric property
    $\tr(\bP_{\mathrm{iso}}\bF^T) = 0$ at every $\bF$;
  \item Ogden reduces to the isochoric neo-Hookean model for
    $(\mu, \alpha) = (\mu, 2)$ and to Mooney--Rivlin for
    $(2c_1, 2), (-2c_2, -2)$; its analytic tangent matches finite
    differences also at coincident principal stretches;
  \item the plane-stress adapter: $P_{33} = 0$ and no out-of-plane shear
    stress, $J = 1$ for incompressible bases, the in-plane dual tangent
    against finite differences (the compressible branch differentiates a
    converged root), $\partial\Psi/\partial\bF = \bP$ in the plane
    (envelope theorem), objectivity under in-plane rotations, the
    small-strain plane-stress moduli $E/(1-\nu^2)$, $E\nu/(1-\nu^2)$ and
    the thickness strain $-\nu/(1-\nu)\,\varepsilon$, and uniaxial tension
    of an incompressible sheet, $P_{11} = \mu(\lambda - \lambda^{-2})$;
  \item the volumetric laws (quadratic, Simo--Taylor, logarithmic,
    $J\ln J$): $u(1) = u'(1) = 0$, $u''(1) = 1$, $u'$ and $u''$ against
    dual and finite-difference derivatives of $u$ and $u'$, and the
    isochoric neo-Hookean and Ogden models through the full decoupled
    checks above with each law; the factory applies the key and the
    coupled models refuse it;
  \item resolution of $(\mu, \kappa)$ from every admissible key combination
    and the refusal of the inadmissible ones;
  \item the viscoelastic adapter (isochoric neo-Hookean with two Maxwell
    branches, evaluated with an explicit history block and step length): at
    rest with $\Delta t = 0$ the stress equals the isochoric neo-Hookean
    model of modulus $\mu + \sum G_i$ ($10^{-16}$); fully relaxed
    ($\Delta t/\tau \to \infty$) it equals the equilibrium branch
    ($3\times10^{-12}$); the updated $\bC^v$ has unit determinant
    ($3\times10^{-16}$); with a history the isochoric stress is deviatoric in
    the Kirchhoff sense and objective ($4\times10^{-15}$),
    $\partial\Psi/\partial\bF = \bP$ at fixed $\bC^v$ ($10^{-16}$), and the
    dual tangent through the update matches finite differences
    ($10^{-10}$); under a held small deviatoric strain the branch stresses
    decay as $2G_i\,\dev\beps\,(1 + \Delta t/\tau_i)^{-n}$ over six steps
    ($1.6\times10^{-6}$, the $O(\epsilon)$ finite-strain terms at
    $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$); every decoupled base rests stress-free; the
    factory wraps the model of \code{branches}, names it, refuses the
    plane-stress adapter and \code{linear\_elastic};
  \item \code{linear\_elastic}: the checks above without objectivity (the
    model is not objective, by construction) and with the small-strain limit
    taken at a strain of $5\%$, where it holds to $10^{-14}$ because the
    model \emph{is} that limit (at the generic $\epsilon = 10^{-7}$ it would
    sit on the $\epsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}/|\Grad\bu|$ cancellation floor,
    $8\times10^{-10}$); $\bP(\bI + \bm W) = \bm 0$ for skew $\bm W$; the dual
    tangent equal to the closed-form $\mathbb C$ at two random $\bF$
    (constant, minor and major symmetries); $\Psi = \tfrac12\bsig:\beps$; the
    split $\bP = \bP_{\mathrm{iso}} + \kappa\tr\beps\,\bI$ with a deviatoric
    $\bP_{\mathrm{iso}}$; under plane stress, stress, thickness strain and
    tangent against the closed forms at a finite strain, for $\nu = 0.3$
    ($\lambda^* = 2\lambda\mu/(\lambda + 2\mu)$) and $\nu = \tfrac12$
    ($\lambda^* = 2\mu$); the factory and its key rules.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discretisation tests}
%============================================================================

\subsection{Patch tests, manufactured solutions, Jacobians (\texttt{test\_solid\_mms})}
On a perturbed quadrilateral mesh an affine displacement $\bu = \bm A\bX + \bm c$
prescribed on the boundary is reproduced exactly (patch test) for both
coupled models.  The smooth manufactured field
$\bu = \alpha(\sin\pi X\sin\pi Y,\ X^2Y(1-Y))$ with the body force
$\bb = -\rho_R^{-1}\Dive\bP(\bF(\bu))$ (divergence by central differences of
the closed-form stress) gives $L^2$ convergence rates of at least
$k + 0.9$ for $k = 1, 2$; on the finest $k = 2$ mesh the last Newton steps
above the round-off floor contract quadratically (order estimate
$\ge 1.5$); the assembled Jacobian matches central differences of the
residual along a random direction at a nonzero state.

\subsection{Mixed formulation (\texttt{test\_mixed})}
Affine patch tests with a constant pressure $p_0 = U'(\det(\bI + \bm A))$
for the decoupled materials at $\nu = 0.45$ and at $\kappa = \infty$, and
at $\nu = 0.45$ with the Simo--Taylor, logarithmic and $J\ln J$ volumetric
laws (exact to $10^{-14}$, quadratic Newton convergence); the block
Jacobian against finite differences ($4\times10^{-9}$), with the pressure
rows checked separately so that the law-dependent block $K_{pu}$ is
exercised; manufactured
solutions with a volume-preserving motion (the composition of two shears
$x = X + a\sin\pi Y$, $y = Y + b\sin\pi x$, $J = 1$, with the smooth
pressure $p = c\sin\pi X\cos\pi Y$) for the incompressible case, rates
$u \ge 3.0$, $p \ge 2.0$, and a compressible field at $\nu = 0.45$ with
$p = \kappa(J-1)$, rates $u \ge 3.4$, $p \ge 2.0$; and agreement of the
mixed and displacement formulations at finite $\kappa$ under refinement.
The pressure is made unique by a manufactured traction on one edge.
\code{--full} adds the $32\times32$ levels.

The same gates for \code{linear\_elastic} (Herrmann's formulation): the
mixed stress at $p = \kappa\tr\beps$ equals the displacement formulation's;
patch tests at an amplitude of $0.3$ with $p_0 = \kappa\tr\bm A$, and with a
traceless $\bm A$ and a free $p_0$ at $\kappa = \infty$, exact in two Newton
steps (the first reaches $4\times10^{-11}\|R_0\|$, the second refines); a
block Jacobian that does not depend on the state (difference exactly zero)
and is symmetric ($4\times10^{-17}$); manufactured solutions at
$\nu = 0.45$ with $p = \kappa\,\dive\bu$ at an amplitude of $0.2$
($|\Grad\bu| \approx 0.6$) and at $\nu = \tfrac12$ with a divergence-free field
from the stream function $\psi = \sin\pi X\sin\pi Y/\pi$, rates
$u \ge 3.0$, $p \ge 2.0$; agreement with the displacement formulation under
refinement; follower pressures, the tangent predictor and $\nu = \tfrac12$
in the displacement formulation refused.

\subsection{Homogeneous deformations (\texttt{test\_homogeneous})}
For the six incompressible models with rubber-like parameters (isochoric
neo-Hookean $\mu = 1$; Mooney--Rivlin $c_1 = 0.4$, $c_2 = 0.1$; Yeoh
$0.5, -0.05, 0.01$; Gent $\mu = 1$, $J_m = 10$; Arruda--Boyce $\mu = 1$,
$N = 5$, with the Pad\'e inverse Langevin and with the series; Ogden $(0.63, 0.0012, -0.01)$, $(1.3, 5.0, -2.0)$):
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item at the material point, the code's Cauchy stress at
    $\bF = \diag(\lambda_a)$ with the analytic mean stress equals the
    analytic principal stresses of the plane-strain, uniaxial, equibiaxial
    and pure-shear states, and the deviatoric stress in simple shear equals
    its spectral closed form with $\sigma_{11} - \sigma_{22} = \gamma\sigma_{12}$;
  \item as finite element solutions of the mixed formulation on $4\times4$
    (plane strain) and $2\times2\times2$ (uniaxial) Q2/Q1 meshes, and of the
    plane-stress displacement formulation for the sheet states uniaxial,
    equibiaxial, pure shear and simple shear on a $4\times4$ Q2 mesh:
    displacement, pressure and every quadrature quantity (Cauchy and first
    Piola--Kirchhoff stress, $J$, von Mises, energy density, thickness
    stretch) in all presentations (both nodal projections, element
    averages, raw quadrature values), exact to solver tolerance with
    quadratic Newton convergence.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Boundary conditions and loading (\texttt{test\_loading})}
On Cook's membrane and unit boxes: staged loading (a traction ramped over
$[0, 0.5]$ and held, a second ramped over $[0.5, 1]$) equals the
single-stage solve, and the state at $t = 0.5$ equals the first traction
alone (path independence); a load-unload table returns to zero; with a
capped Newton the planned step fails and bisection recovers, while without
substepping the failure is reported; the tangent predictor reaches the
same converged state as the default start with fewer Newton iterations
(Cook's membrane with a prescribed edge, 18 to 9; a clamped sheared square
in the mixed formulation with the logarithmic volumetric law at
$\kappa/\mu = 1000$, 17 with a damped step to 6 full steps) and leaves a
traction-driven path unchanged; a programmatic time-dependent
coefficient with the constant schedule reproduces the ramp; the
manufactured problem written entirely as YAML expressions converges at
rate $\ge 2.8$ for $k = 2$, also with only the $x$ component prescribed on
two faces and the exact traction supplied for the other; a traction
expression linear in $t$ equals value plus ramp and one without $t$ is
assembled once; the uniaxial symmetry cube with rollers reproduces the
affine field and closed-form pressure of the full cube, with exactly the
expected number of essential degrees of freedom; rollers on all six faces
under a body force leave no rigid mode; the follower tangent matches
central differences on distorted quadrilateral and hexahedral meshes at a
random state; the follower pressure equals the dead pressure at $\bF = \bI$
and agrees to $O(p^2)$ under a small load; the thick-walled cylinder
inflates as Rivlin says (Section~\ref{sec:rivlincyl}); two-region
materials reproduce the single-material run when the parameters agree, have
a consistent tangent and deform less in the stiff half; and the reactions
equal $P_{11}$ times the face area on the uniaxial cube and balance the
applied resultant on Cook's membrane to $10^{-14}$.

\subsection{Small-strain linear elasticity (\texttt{test\_linear\_elasticity})}
\label{sec:testlinear}
The total Lagrangian kernel with the flux $\bsig(\sym(\bF - \bI))$ must be
the classical linear elasticity operator, and the solve must be linear.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{Oracle.}  On perturbed quadrilateral, triangular, hexahedral
    and tetrahedral meshes, $k = 1, 2$, the assembled Jacobian applied to a
    random vector equals a \code{ParBilinearForm} with MFEM's
    \code{ElasticityIntegrator} and the kernel's quadrature rule to
    $5\times10^{-16}$; it equals the assembled Jacobians of
    \code{neo\_hookean}, \code{st\_venant\_kirchhoff} and
    \code{iso\_neo\_hookean} at $\bu = \bm 0$ (bitwise here), with a control
    away from $\bu = \bm 0$, where they must differ by $O(|\Grad\bu|)$.
  \item \emph{Linearity.}  With Dirichlet data, a traction and a body force:
    one Newton iteration from $\bu = \bm 0$ and from a random start, and at a
    thousand times the load (strains of order one to ten), where the solution
    scales to $10^{-14}$; superposition of the three loads; Clapeyron's
    theorem $2\Psi_{\mathrm{int}} = \bm f_{\mathrm{ext}}\cdot\bu$, all to
    $10^{-10}$ or better.
  \item \emph{Patch tests} at an amplitude of $0.1$ on the four element
    types: $10^{-14}$ in one iteration (the hyperelastic patch tests need
    amplitudes below $10^{-5}$ for that).
  \item \emph{Manufactured solution} with the body force
    $-[(\lambda + \mu)\grad\dive\bu + \mu\,\Delta\bu]$ written out, so that the
    check does not pass through the material's own stress function: $L^2$
    rates 1.97--2.00 ($k = 1$) and 3.00 ($k = 2$).
  \item \emph{Finite-strain limit.}  \code{st\_venant\_kirchhoff} approaches
    it linearly in the load: the relative difference halves with the load
    (ratios 2.00).
  \item \emph{Outputs.}  Five homogeneous Hooke states at a strain of $5\%$
    (three-dimensional uniaxial stress and a general gradient with a rotation
    part, plane strain, plane stress uniaxial and general) solved by finite
    elements; every quantity in every presentation against closed forms built
    from $E$, $\nu$ and the gradient alone, to $6\times10^{-13}$ of a stress
    level of 12.5; end-face reactions $\sigma A$ on the reference area.
    With the finite-strain push-forward left in, this test fails by $3\%$.
  \item \emph{Reactions and input errors.}  Force and moment balance of a
    clamped, loaded square with reference moment arms; follower pressures
    and the tangent predictor refused.
  \item \emph{The floor of the residual.}  A 20:1 beam whose exact first
    step leaves $\|R\|/\|R_0\| = 1.6\times10^{-9}$, above the default Newton

    \code{st\_venant\_kirchhoff} under the same tolerance still fails.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Inertia and time integration (\texttt{test\_dynamics})}
\label{sec:testdynamics}
The dynamic analysis is a decorator over the quasi-static problem (theory
manual, Section ``Elastodynamics''): a constant mass matrix, the step
equation $\bm S(\bm u) + c_M\bM(\bm u - \bm u^*) + \bm h_n = \bm 0$ with
Jacobian $\bK + c_M\bM$, and the load stepper in physical time.  221 checks
in seven seconds.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{Mass.}  On perturbed quadrilateral, triangular, hexahedral and
    tetrahedral meshes, $k = 1, 2$, with two densities by region:
    $\bm 1\cdot\bM\bm 1 = \sum_r\rho_r V_r$ to $2\times10^{-16}$ (the
    reference volumes by a rule that is exact for the trilinear Jacobian),
    symmetry, no coupling between components, and the eliminated copy acting
    as $\bM$ on the free degrees of freedom.
  \item \emph{Free fall.}  No supports, a body force $\bm g$, two densities,
    \code{linear\_elastic} and \code{neo\_hookean}, quadrilaterals and
    tetrahedra: the trapezoidal rule is exact for a constant acceleration, so
    $\bu = \tfrac12\bm g t^2$ at every node on any mesh.  Measured:
    $\bm a_0 = \bm g$ to $4\times10^{-14}$ (the step load is on at $t = 0$),
    $\bu$ to $1.4\times10^{-13}$ over 50 steps, no strain energy.  The test
    fails at once if the mass and the body force disagree on the density of
    a region, which they did before this test existed: a region's
    \code{rho0} was read and ignored.
  \item \emph{Order in $\Delta t$ without spatial error.}
    $\bu = \sin(\omega t)\,\bm U(\bX)$ with $\bm U$ quadratic, which the
    $k = 2$ space on an affine mesh holds exactly, with tractions or with
    prescribed motion on the boundary: rates $1.999$--$2.000$ and
    $2.05 \to 2.005$ for displacement and velocity, for \code{newmark},
    \code{hht} ($0.1$) and \code{generalized\_alpha} ($0.8$).  The mesh is
    $2\times2$ elements and the steps resolve its every mode: the error at a
    fixed time carries the free vibrations that the truncation error
    excites, each with the phase of its numerical frequency, and on a finer
    mesh with coarser steps the ratios scatter between $1.1$ and $3.2$ while
    the errors still fall as $\Delta t^2$.
  \item \emph{Energy and numerical dissipation.}  Linear free vibration: the
    trapezoidal rule keeps the energy to $3\times10^{-14}$ over 500 steps;
    with $\rho_\infty = 0.8$ the energy never exceeds its initial value (it
    is not monotone: the scheme dissipates in a norm of its own).  With
    $\omega\Delta t \gg 1$ for every mode, $\rho_\infty = 0$ leaves
    $10^{-32}$ of the energy after ten steps, $\rho_\infty = 0.5$ makes
    $\ln E_n$ fall with the slope $-1.331$ against $2\ln\rho_\infty =
    -1.386$ (the roots at infinity are a defective $-\rho_\infty$, hence a
    slope and not a ratio), and $\rho_\infty = 1$ keeps it to twelve digits:
    the parameter map of the family.
  \item \emph{Balances.}  A step traction and a moving support, trapezoidal
    rule, linear: kinetic plus internal energy minus the external work of
    load and support is constant to $1.6\times10^{-14}$ of the peak energy
    (an algebraic identity of the scheme), and the reactions plus the
    external force equal the resultant of $\bM\bm a$ to $5\times10^{-13}$.
  \item \emph{Nonlinear path.}  Neo-Hookean block thrown to displacements of
    $0.75$ of its size: Newton order $1.90$ with $\bK + c_M\bM$,
    self-convergence ratios $3.989$ and $3.982$; a manufactured solution in
    space and time converges at the spatial rate $3.015$ under joint
    refinement.
  \item \emph{One Jacobian per run.}  Linear problem, 200 steps: one
    assembly of $\bK$, one of $\bK + c_M\bM$, one AMG setup, and not a digit
    different from 200 of each.  A step made to fail once is halved and the
    run equals the one with the two half steps planned; $\bK + c_M\bM$ is
    then formed three times (for $\Delta t$, $\Delta t/2$ and $\Delta t$
    again).  Without the external work the accepted step does not evaluate
    the full static residual; state and reactions are the same to the last
    digit.
  \item \emph{Stepper.}  $t_{\text{final}} = 10^{-3}$ and $10^{3}$ with 1000
    steps, one of them halved: 1001 accepted steps, the end time is
    $t_{\text{final}}$ exactly, no degenerate step.
  \item \emph{Input.}  The bar input through the library: fields
    \code{velocity} and \code{acceleration} registered and probed, the tip
    displacement equal to $A\cos(\tilde\omega t)$ with the scheme's own
    dispersion $\tan(\tilde\omega\Delta t/2) = \omega\Delta t/2$ to
    $10^{-9}A$.  The parser (85 checks in \file{test\_base}): every error of
    the \code{dynamics} block, schedules in physical time, the default
    schedule of each analysis.
  \item \emph{Mixed formulation.}  A Herrmann problem whose displacement
    (quadratic) and pressure (linear) the Taylor--Hood pair holds exactly, at
    $\nu = 0.4999$ and incompressible: order $2.00$ for $\bu$ and $1.97$ to
    $2.11$ for $p$.  The pressure carries the tolerance of the solves
    multiplied by $c_M = O(\Delta t^{-2})$, so the study uses tight solves
    and 50 to 400 steps; with the usual tolerances that noise ($4\times
    10^{-5}$) hides the order of $p$ from 400 steps on.  Two incompressible
    runs that differ in $p_0$ alone differ at every later step by exactly
    $\rho_\infty$ times the step before ($0.6000$, seven times): the
    pressure mode.  At finite $\kappa$ the initial pressure is
    $\kappa\,\dive\bu_0$ to $6\times10^{-14}$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Finite thermoelasticity and point constraints (\texttt{test\_thermoelastic})}

  \item \textbf{Block Jacobian}, plane strain and axisymmetric, with and

\section{Verification cases (\texttt{apps/input/finite\_elasticity/verification/})}
%============================================================================

Every input in this directory is compared with an independent reference
by a test.  The header of each input states the reference and the
formulas.  Unless noted, the material parameters are dimensionless.

\subsection{Homogeneous deformations of incompressible models}
\label{sec:homogeneous}

\paragraph{Problem.}
A unit square or cube carries an affine displacement $\bu = \bm G\bX$,
$\bm G = \diag(\lambda_a - 1)$, on its constrained faces, written as
expression strings (for example \code{["x", "-0.5*y"]}), with the remaining
faces free; the isochoric neo-Hookean model with \code{incompressible: true}
and $\mu = 1$ ($\mu = 0.5$\,MPa in the three uniaxial inputs, which share
their shear modulus with Section~\ref{sec:compuniaxial}).  The state is homogeneous, so
$\Dive\bP = 0$ holds identically and the affine field with a constant
pressure is the exact solution, which lies in the Taylor--Hood spaces.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}l l l p{4.2cm}@{}}
\toprule
Input & Formulation & Stretches & Constrained faces \\
\midrule
\file{plane\_strain\_neo\_hookean} & mixed, 2D & $(2, \tfrac12, 1)$ & left, right \\
\file{plane\_stress\_neo\_hookean} & plane stress, 2D & $(2, 2^{-1/2}, 2^{-1/2})$ & left, right \\
\file{uniaxial\_neo\_hookean} & mixed, 3D & $(2, 2^{-1/2}, 2^{-1/2})$ & left, right \\
\file{symmetry\_uniaxial\_neo\_hookean} & mixed, 3D & same, octant model & rollers on $X, Y, Z = 0$; $u_x$ on right \\
\file{incompressible\_uniaxial\_iso\_neo\_hookean} & mixed, 3D & $(1.5, 1.5^{-1/2}, 1.5^{-1/2})$, octant model & rollers on $X, Y, Z = 0$; $u_x$ on right \\
\file{equibiaxial\_neo\_hookean} & mixed, 3D & $(1.5, 1.5, 1.5^{-2})$ & left, right, front, back \\
\file{pure\_shear\_neo\_hookean} & mixed, 3D & $(1.5, 1, 1.5^{-1})$ & left, right, front, back \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Reference.}
The closed forms of Appendix~\ref{app:homogeneous}.  With $\beta_a = \lambda_a\,\partial\Psi/\partial\lambda_a$
up to an isotropic term ($\beta_a = 2\Psi_1\lambda_a^2 - 2\Psi_2\lambda_a^{-2}$
for invariant models, $\sum_r\mu_r\lambda_a^{\alpha_r}$ for Ogden), the
principal Cauchy stresses are $\sigma_a = \beta_a - \beta_f$ where $f$ is
the traction-free axis, $p = \tfrac13\sum\sigma_a$,
$\sigma_{vm} = \sqrt{\tfrac32\sum(\sigma_a - p)^2}$, $P_a = \sigma_a/\lambda_a$.
For the neo-Hookean model: uniaxial $\sigma_1 = \mu(\lambda^2 - \lambda^{-1})$;
equibiaxial $\sigma_1 = \sigma_2 = \mu(\lambda^2 - \lambda^{-4})$; pure shear
$\sigma_1 = \mu(\lambda^2 - \lambda^{-2})$, $\sigma_2 = \mu(1 - \lambda^{-2})$;
plane-strain extension $\sigma_{11} = \mu(\lambda^2 - \lambda^{-2})$,
$\sigma_{33} = \mu(1 - \lambda^{-2})$.

\paragraph{Check.}
\file{apps/homogeneous\_compare.py} (\code{make homogeneous}) runs every
input, reads the affine data and the constrained faces from the YAML to
derive the stretches and the free axis, and compares the probed
displacement, pressure or thickness stretch, von Mises stress, $J$ and
every component of $\bsig$ and $\bP$, in the nodal and the element
presentations, with the closed forms; relative tolerance $10^{-8}$
(measured agreement about $10^{-13}$).  Any of the six models runs in the
same inputs by changing the \code{material} line; the script's closed
forms cover all of them.

\subsection{Compressible uniaxial tension}
\label{sec:compuniaxial}

\paragraph{Problem.}
The unit cube with rollers on $X, Y, Z = 0$ and the face $X = 1$ moved by
$0.5\,t$ in $x$ only (stretch $\lambda_1 = 1.5$), all other faces free.
Six inputs with the same small-strain moduli, $\mu = 0.5$\,MPa and
$\nu = 0.45$ ($\kappa = 29/6$\,MPa, $\kappa/\mu \approx 9.7$, compressible
enough for the volumetric laws to differ): coupled neo-Hookean and
Saint Venant--Kirchhoff with $E = 1.45$, $\nu = 0.45$ (displacement
formulation), and the isochoric neo-Hookean model with $\mu = 0.5$,
$\kappa = 29/6$ in the mixed formulation with each volumetric law:
quadratic (\file{\ldots\_mixed}), \code{simo\_taylor}, \code{j\_log\_j} and
\code{logarithmic} (the mixed constraint with $u'' \ne 1$).
\file{incompressible\_uniaxial\_iso\_neo\_hookean}
(Section~\ref{sec:homogeneous}) is their limit $\kappa = \infty$.  A
seventh input, \file{compressible\_uniaxial\_gent\_compressible\_summit},
uses SUMMIT's compressible Gent model with $\mu = 0.5$, $\kappa = 29/6$ and
$J_m = 100$ (displacement formulation); its $\kappa$ scales a quartic
penalty and is not a small-strain bulk modulus, so that block is far more
compressible than the others.

\paragraph{Reference.}
The state is homogeneous with $\bF = \diag(\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_2)$
and the lateral stretch fixed by $\sigma_{22} = 0$.  The test solves
$P_{22}(\diag(\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_2)) = 0$ for $\lambda_2$ with
the material's own stress function (which carries the volumetric law), then
forms $P_{11}$, $\sigma_{11}$ and $J$.

\paragraph{Check} (\file{test\_verification}).  Axial displacement to
$10^{-10}$, lateral displacements to $10^{-8}$, $P_{11}$, $\sigma_{11}$ and
$J$ to $10^{-7}$ relative, and the lateral nominal stresses vanish to
$10^{-7}P_{11}$.

\paragraph{Large stretch} (\file{apps/uniaxial\_plots.py},
\file{apps/neo\_hookean\_compare.py}).  The first script drives every
uniaxial input of this and the previous section to $\lambda_1 = 8$ in 70
load steps (Saint Venant--Kirchhoff to 0.95 of $\sqrt{1 + 1/\nu}$, where its
lateral stretch $\lambda_2^2 = 1 - \nu(\lambda_1^2 - 1)$ vanishes), reads the
ParaView series back and plots $P_{11}(\lambda_1)$ on an analytical curve
coded independently of \file{src/}: $\lambda_2$ by bisection of
$P_{22} = 0$ with
$P_{aa} = \mu(\lambda_a - \lambda_a^{-1}) + \lambda\ln J/\lambda_a$ (coupled
neo-Hookean) or
$P_{aa} = \mu J^{-2/3}(\lambda_a - I_1/(3\lambda_a)) + U'(J)J/\lambda_a$
(decoupled), the closed forms $\lambda_2^2 = 1 - \nu(\lambda_1^2 - 1)$,
$P_{11} = E\lambda_1(\lambda_1^2 - 1)/2$ for Saint Venant--Kirchhoff,
$P_{aa} = \mu J_m\lambda_a/(J_m - I_1 + 3) + (2\kappa A^3(J^2-1) - \mu)/\lambda_a$
for the SUMMIT Gent model (which shows the Gent upturn towards its locking
stretch, $I_1 - 3 = 61.7$ of $J_m = 100$ at $\lambda_1 = 8$), and
$P_{11} = \mu(\lambda_1 - \lambda_1^{-2})$ when incompressible.  Measured
agreement is $3\times10^{-12}$ relative or better at every step, and the
nodal $P_{11}$ is uniform to $2\times10^{-11}$.  The second script overlays
the neo-Hookean responses: at $\lambda_1 = 8$ the decoupled models lie 33\%
(quadratic) to 51\% (logarithmic, $J = 2.9$, beyond the peak of $\ln J/J$
at $J = e$) below the incompressible curve, while the coupled model, for
which $P_{22} = 0$ gives $P_{11} = \mu(\lambda_1 - \lambda_2^2/\lambda_1)$,
stays on it.

\paragraph{Problem.}
An exact displacement is prescribed on the whole boundary and the body
force $\bb = -\rho_R^{-1}\Dive\bP(\bF(\bu))$ that makes it the solution is
supplied as expression strings generated by sympy (expanded, no
simplification).  Saint Venant--Kirchhoff with $E = 250$, $\nu = 0.3$,
$\rho_R = 2.5$ unless noted, $k = 2$.

\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}l l p{7.8cm}@{}}
\toprule
Input & Mesh & Exact displacement \\
\midrule
\file{mms\_2d\_plane\_strain} & \file{square.msh} & $0.05\,(\sin\pi x\sin\pi y,\ x^2y(1-y))$ \\
\file{mms\_3d\_hex} & \file{cube.msh} & $0.05\,(\sin\pi x\sin\pi y\sin\pi z,\ x^2y(1-y)z,\ xz(1-z)\cos\tfrac{\pi y}{2})$ \\

\file{mms\_3d\_mixed} & \file{cube.msh} & $0.05\,(\sin\pi y\sin\pi z,\ z(1-z),\ 0)$; isochoric neo-Hookean, $\mu = 40$, $\kappa = 400$, mixed formulation \\

The mixed field has a strictly upper-triangular gradient, so $J = 1$
exactly and the exact pressure $\kappa(J - 1)$ vanishes; it is applied in
four increments because on fine meshes the exact boundary data applied at
once inverts the elements next to the boundary while the interior is still
at rest.

\paragraph{Check.}
$L^2$ displacement error under \code{serial\_refine} 1, 2, 3 (two
dimensions, \file{test\_loading}) or 1, 2 (three dimensions,
\file{test\_verification}); the rate $\log_2(e_{h}/e_{h/2})$ must be at
least 2.8, i.e.\ third order for $k = 2$.

\subsection{Rivlin's inflation of a thick-walled cylinder}
\label{sec:rivlincyl}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Quarter annulus $A = 1$, $B = 2$ (\file{annulus.msh}, second-order
geometry), plane strain, incompressible neo-Hookean with $\mu = 1$, mixed
formulation; rollers on the two symmetry lines through component-wise
Dirichlet data; follower pressure $P = 0.3$ per unit current area on the
inner surface, ramped over the load path.

\paragraph{Reference} (Rivlin; Ogden, \emph{Non-linear Elastic
Deformations}, Section 5.3.1).  With $a, b$ the deformed radii and
$b^2 = B^2 - A^2 + a^2$,
\begin{equation}
  P = \mu\Bigl[\ln\frac{B}{A} - \ln\frac{b}{a} + \frac{A^2 - a^2}{2}\Bigl(\frac1{b^2} - \frac1{a^2}\Bigr)\Bigr];
  \qquad P = 0.3 \ \Rightarrow\ a = 1.28158,\ b = 2.15463 .
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Check} (\file{test\_loading}).  On the base mesh and one
refinement: inner and outer radii within $0.2\%$ of the inflation
$(a - A)$, $(b - B)$; the radial displacement is the same at $(A, 0)$ and
$(0, A)$ to $10^{-6}$; the rollers hold the symmetry lines to $10^{-12}$;
the final Newton solve takes at most six iterations (quadratic convergence
with the follower tangent); and the error decreases under refinement.  The
README quotes the agreement as $10^{-6}$ on the refined mesh.

\subsection{Green--Zerna inflation of a thick-walled sphere}
\label{sec:greenzerna}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Octant of a spherical shell $A = 10$, $B = 11$ (\file{sphere\_octant.msh},
curved second-order tetrahedra), incompressible neo-Hookean $\mu = 1$,
mixed formulation, rollers on the three symmetry planes, follower pressure
$P = 0.100975807337556$ on the inner surface.

\paragraph{Reference} (Green and Zerna; Ogden).  With $\lambda_a = a/A$,
$\lambda_b = b/B$ and $b^3 = B^3 - A^3 + a^3$,
\begin{equation}
  P = \frac{\mu}{2}\Bigl[\Bigl(\frac4{\lambda_b} + \frac1{\lambda_b^4}\Bigr) - \Bigl(\frac4{\lambda_a} + \frac1{\lambda_a^4}\Bigr)\Bigr],
\end{equation}
and the chosen $P$ gives $\lambda_a = 1.2$ exactly ($a = 12$,
$b = 12.72190$), below the pressure maximum of this shell near
$\lambda_a = 1.4$.  The test inverts the relation by bisection.

\paragraph{Check} (\file{test\_verification}).  Inner and outer radii
within $0.5\%$ of the inflation; the inner radius on the $z$ axis equals
that on the $x$ axis to $10^{-3}(a - A)$ (spherical symmetry).

\subsection{Rivlin's torsion of a solid cylinder}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Solid cylinder $a = 12.7$, $L = 25.4$ (\file{cylinder\_torsion.msh}, curved
second-order tetrahedra), incompressible neo-Hookean $\mu = 1$, mixed
formulation.  The bottom face is clamped and the top face rotated rigidly
by $\theta = 1$\,rad with $u_z = 0$ ($\bu = \bR(\psi z)\bX - \bX$ on the
face, twist $\psi = \theta/L$, rim shear $\psi a = 0.5$) in 20 increments; a
larger first increment leaves the elements under the rotated face inverted
before Newton starts.

\paragraph{Reference.}
Rivlin's universal solution holds exactly: $\bu = \bR(\psi z)\bX - \bX$
throughout, the lateral surface is traction free by itself, and in
cylindrical components with $\gamma = \psi r$
\begin{gather}
  \sigma_{rz} = 0, \quad \sigma_{\theta z} = \mu\psi r, \quad
  \sigma_{rr} = \sigma_{zz} = -\tfrac12\mu\psi^2(a^2 - r^2), \quad
  \sigma_{\theta\theta} = \sigma_{rr} + \mu\psi^2r^2, \\
  p = \sigma_{rr} + \tfrac13\mu\psi^2r^2, \qquad
  \sigma_{vm} = \mu\sqrt{\gamma^4 + 3\gamma^2}.
\end{gather}

\paragraph{Check} (\file{test\_verification}).  Displacement field to
$2\times10^{-3}$; on the mid-plane the shear stress $\sigma_{\theta z}$
and the von Mises stress to $2\%$, the normal stresses and the pressure to
$3\%$ of the stress scale, and the absence of $r\theta$ and $rz$ shear to
$2\%$ (curved P2 tetrahedra).

\subsection{Euler--Bernoulli cantilever in the linear limit}

\paragraph{Problem.}
A $10\times1\times1$ beam (\file{beam.msh}, $20\times2\times2$ hexahedra,
$k = 2$) clamped at $x = 0$ with a downward shear traction on $x = L$ small
enough that $\max|\Grad\bu| \approx 5\times10^{-5}$; compressible
neo-Hookean, and the same problem with Saint Venant--Kirchhoff.  The
systems AMG options are selected in the input because the elasticity
options stall on this slender mesh; the test also checks that the
automatic fallback converges.

\paragraph{Reference.}
Euler--Bernoulli tip deflection $PL^3/(3EI) = 3.2\times10^{-4}$.

\paragraph{Check} (\file{test\_benchmarks}).  $\max|\Grad\bu| \le 10^{-4}$
(the load is in the linear regime); tip deflection within $0.15\%$ of the
beam value; the two materials agree in the linear limit.

\subsection{Euler buckling of a clamped column (Southwell plot)}

\paragraph{Problem.}
A $1\times1\times20$ column (\file{column\_euler.msh}, $2\times2\times40$
hexahedra) carrying the first-mode imperfection
$x, y \mathrel{-}= 0.0025\,(1 - \cos 2\pi z/20)$; compressible neo-Hookean
$E = 100$, $\nu = 0.3$.  The base is clamped, the top held in $x, y$ and
shortened by $u_z = -0.12\,t$, an axial strain up to $0.6\%$, about $73\%$
of the Euler strain of the clamped-clamped column,
$\varepsilon_{cr} = 4\pi^2I/(AL^2) = 8.22\times10^{-3}$.

\paragraph{Reference.}
Below the limit point the mid-height lateral deflection of an imperfect
column grows as $u = \delta_0\,(P/P_{cr})/(1 - P/P_{cr})$ (Southwell), so
$1/u$ is linear in $1/\varepsilon$ with slope $\varepsilon_{cr}/\delta_0$
and intercept $-1/\delta_0$.

\paragraph{Check} (\file{test\_verification}).  A least-squares fit of
$1/u$ against $1/\varepsilon$ over the load steps recovers
$\varepsilon_{cr}$ within $5\%$ of the Euler value and the imperfection
amplitude $\delta_0$ within $15\%$ of the geometric one (0.005).

\subsection{Kirsch's plate with a circular hole}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Quarter of a $40\times40$ plate with a hole of radius $a = 1$
(\file{plate\_hole\_2d.msh}, quadrilaterals of size 0.04 at the hole,
curved second-order geometry), plane stress, compressible neo-Hookean
$E = 1000$, $\nu = 0.3$; rollers on the symmetry lines, far-field traction
$\sigma_0 = 0.1$ on $x = 20$ (strain $10^{-4}$, linear regime).

\paragraph{Reference.}
Kirsch's infinite-plate solution: $\sigma_{xx} = 3\sigma_0$ at the hole top
$(0, a)$, $\sigma_{yy} = -\sigma_0$ at the hole side $(a, 0)$, and along
the $y$ axis $\sigma_{xx}/\sigma_0 = 1 + a^2/(2y^2) + 3a^4/(2y^4)$.  The
finite width $W = 20a$ raises the concentration by a few tenths of a
percent (Howland).

\paragraph{Check} (\file{test\_verification}).  Probed Cauchy stresses
$\sigma_{xx}$, $\sigma_{yy}$ within $2\%$ of $3\sigma_0$ at the probe
points, and no shear stress on the symmetry axes to $1\%$ of $3\sigma_0$.

\section{Benchmarks (\texttt{apps/input/finite\_elasticity/cooks\_membrane/})}
%============================================================================

\paragraph{Problem.}
Cook's membrane: the quadrilateral with corners $(0,0)$, $(48,44)$,
$(48,60)$, $(0,44)$ (\file{cook.msh}), plane strain, left edge clamped,
uniform upward shear traction on the right edge, $k = 2$.  Three inputs:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}l l p{5.6cm} l@{}}
\toprule
Input & Formulation & Material & Traction (resultant) \\
\midrule
\file{cook} & displacement & neo-Hookean $E = 250$, $\nu = 0.3$ & 3.75 (60) \\
\file{cook\_incompressible} & mixed Q2--Q1 & isochoric neo-Hookean $\mu = 80.194$, $\kappa = \infty$ & 6.25 (100) \\
\file{cook\_nearly\_incompressible} & mixed Q2--Q1 & the same with $\nu = 0.4999$ ($\kappa = 4\times10^5$) & 6.25 (100) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Reference.}
There is no closed form; the benchmark is a literature standard for
locking and mesh convergence.  The test performs a self-convergence study
(the base $4\times4$ mesh and four uniform refinements up to $64\times64$
for the compressible case, up to $32\times32$ for the incompressible one)
and holds the finest corner displacement as a frozen regression value.

\paragraph{Check} (\file{test\_benchmarks}).  Monotone self-convergence of
the vertical displacement of the top-right corner $(48, 60)$; the frozen
values $u_y = 4.905891700497$ (compressible, $64\times64$; $30.7\%$ of the
16\,mm edge) and $u_y = 6.930412595013$ (incompressible, $32\times32$;
$43.3\%$) reproduced to $10^{-8}$ relative, serially and with four ranks.
The final step of \code{make test}, \code{--cook-ratio-gate}, asserts the
plan's requirement that successive differences shrink by at least three
per refinement.  That threshold is \emph{not} met (measured ratios 2.34,
2.45, 2.31): uniform refinement is limited by the singularity at the
108-degree clamped-free corner, so the point value converges at about
$h^{1.3}$.  Everything before that step is green; the threshold is kept
as written rather than relaxed, so \code{make test} ends with this known
failure.

\section{Small-strain cases (\texttt{apps/input/linear\_elasticity/})}
\label{sec:linearcases}
%============================================================================

The inputs of this directory use \code{model: linear\_elastic} and are
checked by \file{tests/test\_linear\_verification} (about 30 s).  Every
displacement solve takes one Newton iteration and every mixed one at most
two; \code{newton.rtol} is $10^{-8}$ because it only accepts the linear
solve (theory manual, Section on the solution of a linear problem), and the
accuracy is set by \code{linear.rtol} $= 10^{-13}$.  Several cases are the
small-strain counterparts of a finite-strain input and are compared with
it: the hyperelastic ``linear limit'' runs have to keep $|\Grad\bu|$ near
$10^{-4}$ and their Newton tolerance above the round-off floor of their
stress evaluation, whereas the amplitude of a linear run is free.

\subsection{Lam\'e's thick-walled cylinder}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Quarter annulus $a = 1$, $b = 2$ (\file{annulus.msh}, second-order arcs,
$16\times32$ quadrilaterals after two refinements, $k = 2$), plane strain,
rollers on the symmetry lines, dead pressure $P = 10$ on the inner surface;
$E = 1000$ with $\nu = 0.3$ in the displacement formulation
(\file{lame\_cylinder}) and $\nu = \tfrac12$ in the mixed one
(\file{lame\_cylinder\_incompressible}).

\paragraph{Reference.}
With $A = Pa^2/(b^2 - a^2)$ \cite{timoshenko},
$u_r = \frac{1+\nu}{E}A\bigl[(1-2\nu)r + b^2/r\bigr]$,
$\sigma_{rr} = A(1 - b^2/r^2)$, $\sigma_{\theta\theta} = A(1 + b^2/r^2)$,
$\sigma_{zz} = 2\nu A$.  For $\nu = \tfrac12$ the mean stress
$\tfrac12(\sigma_{rr} + \sigma_{\theta\theta}) = A$ is uniform, so the
pressure unknown must be the constant $A = 10/3$.

\paragraph{Check} (\file{test\_linear\_verification}).
$u_r(a)$, $u_r(b)$ and $u_r$ in the wall to $10^{-5}$ (measured
$3\times10^{-6}$, the floor of the second-order arcs, which refinement
keeps).  The pointwise stresses and strains of the projected fields at
$r = 1.25, 1.5, 1.75$ on the diagonal converge with $h^2$: error ratios
3.90, 4.04, 4.02 over three refinements, $0.26\%$ of $P$ at the input's
level.  Incompressible: $u_r$ to $4\times10^{-6}$, the pressure unknown equal
to $A$ to $3\times10^{-6}$ in the wall and $10^{-4}$ in the corner elements,
$\sigma_{zz} = A$, and $\tr\beps$ small against the strains (the constraint
holds weakly, so pointwise it is $O(h^2)$, not zero).
\emph{Locking record} at $\nu = 0.4999$ on the same mesh, error of $u_r(a)$:
displacement formulation $k = 1$: $50\%$; $k = 2$: $2.8\times10^{-4}$; mixed
Q2--Q1: $4\times10^{-6}$.

\subsection{Lam\'e's thick-walled sphere}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Octant $a = 10$, $b = 11$ (\file{sphere\_octant.msh}, curved second-order
tetrahedra), rollers on the three symmetry planes, dead pressure $P = 1$
inside, $E = 1000$, $\nu = 0.3$ (\file{lame\_sphere}).

\paragraph{Reference.}
With $A = Pa^3/(b^3 - a^3)$,
$u_r = \frac{A}{E}\bigl[(1-2\nu)r + (1+\nu)b^3/(2r^2)\bigr]$,
$\sigma_{rr} = A(1 - b^3/r^3)$, $\sigma_{\theta\theta} = A(1 + b^3/(2r^3))$.

\paragraph{Check.}
$u_r(a)$, $u_r(b)$ and $u_r(a)$ on the $z$ axis to $3\times10^{-4}$ (measured
$7\times10^{-5}$); the stresses at $r = 10.5$ on the diagonal to $1\%$ of the
hoop stress, no shear on the radial direction.

\subsection{Kirsch's plate, the cantilever}

\paragraph{Problem.}
\file{kirsch\_plate\_with\_hole} and \file{euler\_bernoulli\_cantilever3d}:
the finite-strain inputs of the same names with \code{linear\_elastic}, the
plate under $\sigma_0 = 1$ so that the probed stresses are the concentration
factors, the beam under a thousand times the load.

\paragraph{Check.}
Kirsch's stresses to $2\%$ of $3\sigma_0$ (the finite width dominates), no
shear on the axes, $\sigma_{zz} = 0$ to $10^{-10}$ (plane stress), and
agreement with the neo-Hookean run at a strain of $10^{-4}$ to
$5\times10^{-4}$ (measured $9\times10^{-5}$).  Cantilever: tip deflection
within $0.2\%$ of $PL^3/(3EI)$ (measured $0.15\%$); compliance equal to that
of the small-load neo-Hookean run to $10^{-7}$ (measured $10^{-9}$: the
transverse deflection is odd in the load, so the nonlinear correction is
second order in $|\Grad\bu| = 5\times10^{-5}$); clamp force and moment
$PL$ with reference arms to $10^{-9}$.

\subsection{Manufactured solutions}

\paragraph{Problem.}
The displacement fields of the finite-strain manufactured inputs on the

\subsection{Two materials by regions}

\paragraph{Problem.}
\file{spherical\_inclusion}: the octant of a cube with a centred spherical
inclusion (\file{inclusion.msh}), matrix $E = 100$, inclusion ten times
stiffer through \code{material.regions}, uniaxial strain of $1\%$ with
rollers on every face.

\paragraph{Reference.}
The apparent constrained modulus $M = F/(L^2\varepsilon) = 2\Psi/(V\varepsilon^2)$
lies between the Reuss and Voigt averages of $\lambda + 2\mu$ with the
volume fraction $f = \pi a^3/(6L^3)$ \cite{hill}: $143.04 < M < 213.91$.
Both bounds hold for the discrete solution (affine fields are in the space;
the displacement method overestimates the energy).

\paragraph{Check.}
$M = 148.86$ from the reaction and from the energy (equal to $10^{-9}$),
within the bounds; with the base's parameters in the region the solution is
the homogeneous uniaxial-strain field and $M = \lambda + 2\mu$ to $10^{-10}$.

\subsection{Cook's membrane in the small-strain theory}

\paragraph{Problem.}
The membrane as it was posed \cite{cook}: plane stress, $E = 1$,

\subsection{Step load on the bar: d'Alembert's wave}
\paragraph{Problem} (\file{bar\_step\_load.yaml}, \file{bar.msh}).  The same

\subsection{Radial oscillation of an incompressible tube (mixed $u$--$p$)}
\label{sec:knowles}
\paragraph{Problem} (\file{knowles\_tube\_oscillation.yaml}).  Plane strain,
the quarter annulus $A = 1 \le R \le B = 2$ of the Lam\'e and Rivlin cases
with rollers on the symmetry lines and both surfaces free of traction,
incompressible \code{iso\_neo\_hookean}, $\mu = 1$, $\rho_R = 1$,
\code{formulation: mixed}, $\rho_\infty = 0.8$.  The tube starts undeformed
with the isochoric radial velocity $\bm v_0 = k\bX/|\bX|^2$, $k = 0.4$; with
free surfaces no follower load is needed.

\paragraph{Reference} (Knowles~\cite{knowles}).  Incompressibility leaves
the motion one degree of freedom, $r^2 = R^2 + c(t)$, so that
$\dot r = \dot c/2r$ and $\ddot r = \ddot c/2r - \dot c^2/4r^3$.  The
spatial radial balance is
$\rho_R\ddot r = \partial_r\sigma_{rr} + (\sigma_{rr} - \sigma_{\theta\theta})/r$,
and for the neo-Hookean solid $\bsig = -q\bI + \mu\bB$ with
$\lambda_\theta = r/R = \lambda$, $\lambda_r = 1/\lambda$ gives
$\sigma_{\theta\theta} - \sigma_{rr} = \mu(\lambda^2 - \lambda^{-2})$.
Integrating from the inner radius $a$, where $\sigma_{rr} = 0$, with
$\int\mu(\lambda^2 - \lambda^{-2})\ud r/r = \tfrac{\mu}{2}[\ln((s - c)/s) -
c/s]$, $s = r^2$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \sigma_{rr}(r) ={}& \rho_R\Bigl[\frac{\ddot c}{2}\ln\frac ra
  + \frac{\dot c^2}{8}\Bigl(\frac1{r^2} - \frac1{a^2}\Bigr)\Bigr] \\
  &+ \frac\mu2\Bigl[\ln\frac{(r^2 - c)\,a^2}{r^2A^2} - \frac c{r^2} + \frac c{a^2}\Bigr],
  \qquad a^2 = A^2 + c .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:knowlesstress}
\end{equation}
The outer surface is free as well, $\sigma_{rr}(b) = 0$ with
$b^2 = B^2 + c$, which is Knowles' equation for $c(t)$,
\begin{equation}
  \rho_R\Bigl[\frac{\ddot c}{2}\ln\frac ba
  - \frac{\dot c^2}{8}\Bigl(\frac1{a^2} - \frac1{b^2}\Bigr)\Bigr]
  = -\frac\mu2\Bigl[\ln\frac{B^2a^2}{A^2b^2} + c\Bigl(\frac1{a^2} - \frac1{b^2}\Bigr)\Bigr],
  \qquad c(0) = 0,\ \dot c(0) = 2k .
\end{equation}
Small oscillations have $\omega^2 = 2\mu(A^{-2} - B^{-2})/(\rho_R\ln(B/A))$,
a period of $4.27$; at this amplitude the inner radius swings between $0.91$
and $1.28$ with the period $4.2428$.  The pressure unknown of the mixed
formulation is $p = \sigma_{rr} - \mu\dev(\bB)_{rr}$ with
$\dev(\bB)_{rr} = (2R^2/r^2 - r^2/R^2 - 1)/3$ (the plane-strain $\bB$ has
$B_{zz} = 1$), so \eqref{eq:knowlesstress} gives it at every material
radius.  The test integrates the equation with a Runge--Kutta scheme of its
own (40 substeps per time step), the script with \code{scipy}.

\paragraph{Check.}  Inner displacement: first peak $0.280754$ against
$0.280749$, largest difference over two periods $2.3\times10^{-3}$ of it
(gate $5\times10^{-3}$; the outer wall $6\times10^{-4}$), period $4.2431$
against $4.2428$.  Pressure at mid-wall, $R = 1.5$, where it is carried by
the inertia of the material inside it (at the wall the free surface ties it
to the kinematics): within $2.0\times10^{-3}$ of its largest value $0.3385$
after the start-up (gate 2 percent; $t > 0.5$).  The start-up is visible in
the pressure of the first steps: the initial pressure of an incompressible
material is taken as zero and the error returns with the factor
$-\rho_\infty$ (Section~\ref{sec:testdynamics}).

\paragraph{The linear solver.}  This case is also the measurement behind the
inertial part of the Schur complement approximation (theory manual, saddle-point
solver).  Outer FGMRES iterations of the first Newton solve; the
quasi-static inflation on the same mesh needs 18:

\midrule

\section{Kirsch's plate with exact boundary data (\texttt{plate\_with\_hole/})}
\label{sec:platewithhole}
%============================================================================

\paragraph{Problem.}
The exercise of \file{myapps/plate\_with\_hole}: a quarter plate $[0, 3R]^2$
with a quarter hole of radius $R = 0.01$ at the origin
(\file{plate\_with\_a\_hole.msh}, the exercise's own mesh: 10900 nodes,
straight-sided triangles), plane strain, \code{linear\_elastic} with
$\mu = 7\times10^{10}$, $\nu = 0.3$.  The displacement of the infinite plate
under the far-field stress $\sigma_\infty = 0.01\mu$ is prescribed on the four
outer edges and the hole is traction free.  The exercise is a driver of its
own; here it is the input \file{plate\_linear} of the general executable,
with the Dirichlet data written as expressions in $x$ and $y$.

\paragraph{Reference.}
Kirsch's solution \cite{kirsch,timoshenko} with $k = 3 - 4\nu$,
\begin{align*}
  u_r &= \frac{\sigma_\infty}{8\mu r}\Bigl[(k-1)r^2 + 2R^2 + 2\bigl(R^2(k+1) + r^2 - R^4/r^2\bigr)\cos2\theta\Bigr], \\
  u_\theta &= -\frac{\sigma_\infty}{4\mu r}\bigl[r^2 + R^2(k-1) + R^4/r^2\bigr]\sin2\theta ,
\end{align*}
and the stresses of Section~\ref{sec:linearcases}.  Because the exact field
is imposed on the outer boundary there is no finite-width error (the case
\file{kirsch\_plate\_with\_hole} of Section~\ref{sec:linearcases} pulls a finite
plate by a traction and carries one): the finite element solution converges
to the closed form, up to the geometry of the mesh, whose hole is a polygon
with a chord sag of $10^{-4}R$ that uniform refinement keeps.

\paragraph{Check} (\file{test\_linear\_verification};
\file{apps/plate\_with\_hole\_compare.py --check} in \code{make test};
\code{make plate\_with\_hole} for the plot).
The expressions of the input equal an independent implementation of the
closed form to $10^{-13}$.  Relative $L^2$ error of the displacement
$5.3\times10^{-5}$ for $k = 1$ (gate $10^{-4}$) and $1.4\times10^{-5}$ for
$k = 2$ (gate $3\times10^{-5}$, the geometry floor); $\sigma_{xx}$ just
inside the top of the hole and $\sigma_{yy}$ at its side within $1\%$ and
$0.3\%$ of $3\sigma_\infty$; $\sigma_{zz} = \nu(\sigma_{xx} + \sigma_{yy})$.
The script reads the ParaView output back and compares every node:
root-mean-square errors $5.5\times10^{-5}$ (displacement) and
$2.6\times10^{-3}\sigma_\infty$ ($\sigma_{xx}$) at $k = 1$,
$9.1\times10^{-4}$ after one refinement and $2.1\times10^{-4}$ at $k = 2$;
concentration factor $2.991$, $2.997$, $2.998$.  With \code{neo\_hookean} and
the same moduli the displacement differs from Kirsch's by
$2.9\times10^{-4}$: at these strains geometric nonlinearity is five times
the discretisation error.

Both codes use Taylor--Hood elements (P2--P1 on tetrahedra, Q2--Q1 on

\paragraph{Differences from the reference that change the numbers.}
The reference caps the relative chain stretch
$\bar\lambda/\lambda_L$ of the Pad\'e form at $0.95$ and this code does not
(the cap is a chain stretch $\bar\lambda = 4.86$; the homogeneous cube of
case 01, the most stretched, ends at $4.40$).  Cases 02 and 08 keep the
quadratic law $p = K(J-1)$, because with the logarithmic law both runs
fail, the shear at $t = 0.064$ for any increment size and the column at
0.8\,mm of shortening on either post-buckling branch: both have clamped
faces whose corners are stress singularities that this mesh and quadrature
resolve sharply, the Q1 pressure cannot follow them, $J$ runs away there
($0.7$ to $1.5$ in the column with the pressure at 25\,kPa, $p/K = 10^{-4}$,
and without bound in the sheared block), and the constraint $\ln J/J$ of
that law loses its definiteness (its tangent $K(1 - \ln J)/J^2$ is $0.18\,K$
at $J = 1.67$ and zero at $J = e$), so that Newton stalls at
$|R| = 10^{-2}$; with the quadratic law both converge in three iterations
per step.  The column also needs the reference's 100 steps: with 50, the
tangent predictor carries the nearly straight column across the critical
load onto the unstable straight branch (a mid-height deflection of
0.005\,mm at four times the Euler load), which Newton loses at 0.8\,mm of
shortening.  Hexahedra replace tetrahedra on the boxes, and a failed
increment is bisected instead of ending the run (cases 05 and 06 stop early
in the reference).

\paragraph{Plots.}
\file{apps/anand\_plots.py} reproduces the result plots of the reference
pages from the ParaView output of the runs, following the \file{.pvd} of each
case. The probes are the nodal fields interpolated at the probe points of the
input. The reactions are not part of the output: the force and the moment of
a Dirichlet entry are computed as
$\int \bP\bN\,dA_R$ and
$\int (\bX + \bu) \times \bP\bN\,dA_R$ over the
faces of its physical groups, with the nodal \code{pk1\_stress}, the
prescribed components only, by Gauss quadrature of the Lagrange interpolants
of the output cells. Since that stress is recovered from the quadrature
points, the force differs from the reaction of the code (the residual summed
over the prescribed dofs) where the loaded face meets free faces or clamped
corners: $+9.7\%$ for case 04 and $-8\%$ for case 08 at the end of the run,
$+3.5\%$ for 02, $0.6\%$ for 03, $0.1\%$ for 09 and 10, round-off for the
homogeneous cube.  So the script takes the reactions from
\file{<paraview>/reactions.csv} when the run wrote it
(\code{output.reactions: true}), or else from the code's log beside the
output (\file{logs/<case>.log}, as \file{logs/run\_set.sh} writes it); with \code{--logs} it reads probes and
reactions from the logs alone (\file{<dir>/<case>.log}). It overlays an analytical curve where one exists:
the homogeneous incompressible and nearly incompressible Arruda--Boyce
response for the uniaxial and shear blocks, Rivlin's universal torsion for torque and
axial force by quadrature in $r$, the incompressible thick-walled cylinder
and sphere inflation by quadrature with their limit points, the Euler load
$4\pi^2EI/L^2$ for the clamped column, and the matrix-only curve for the
inclusion.  It also overlays the reference's own results:
\file{finite\_elasticity/reference/<case>.csv} are the histories that the
reference's notebooks record while they run (their \code{timeHist} arrays,
the probed displacement and the force or pressure of each plot, plus the
reaction of the loaded face, the residual summed over its dofs, which the

\section{Scalar transport cases (\texttt{apps/input/scalar\_transport/})}
\label{sec:scalarcases}
%============================================================================

The five convection--diffusion verification drivers of
\file{myapps/convection\_diffusion} as inputs of the scalar transport
executable (theory manual, Section~``Scalar transport''), on copies of

same triangulation, order and steps the drivers' stock integrators

order $2p + 3$ does, so the discrete problems coincide once the source is
integrated with the driver's rule (\code{DomainLFIntegrator}'s order $2p$;
the nonlinear driver's hand-written integrators use $2p + 2$), which the
comparisons set through \code{transport.quadrature\_order}; the inputs
themselves use the framework's rule.  The error histories then agree to the
drivers' Krylov tolerance: the two transient linear drivers were run with
their tolerances tightened (rtol $10^{-13}$, atol $10^{-18}$), since with
their own atol of $10^{-12}$ the Pe = 1 history, whose error is
$1.6\times10^{-5}$ at $t = 1$, drifted by $4\times10^{-4}$.

step of the nonlinear case), and with \code{--check} asserts every
compared error at $10^{-5}$ (measured: Pe = 1 $3.8\times10^{-7}$, Pe = 10
$3\times10^{-9}$, Pe = 100 $9\times10^{-11}$, square $3\times10^{-9}$,
disk $4.7\times10^{-7}$, the transient manufactured solution $4\times10^{-10}$).
\file{tests/test\_scalar\_verification.cpp} (\code{make test}, about

wiring, adds the nonlinear case (whose series is coded there), and

\subsection{Transient convection--diffusion at three P\'eclet numbers}
\file{convection\_diffusion\_peclet\_\{1,10,100\}.yaml}, the driver
\file{linear\_convection\_diffusion\_1D.cpp}: $\partial_t c + \bm\beta\cdot\grad c - \mathrm{Pe}^{-1}\Delta c = 0$
on the unit square with $\bm\beta = (1, 0)$, $c = 0$ at $t = 0$, the exact
solution of the half-line problem prescribed on $x = 0$ and $x = 1$

the other sides; $p = 3$ on the 938-triangle mesh, $\Delta t = 10^{-3}$ to
$t = 1$, one input per P\'eclet number (the driver runs the three in one
go).  Check: the $L^2$ error histories against the driver's to $10^{-5}$
(measured above); the framework's own: at Pe = 1 the error at $t = 1$ is
first order in $\Delta t$ (1000, 500, 250 steps: rates $1.001$, $1.002$; at
$p = 3$ the temporal error dominates, so no spatial rate is observable
there); at Pe = 10 and 100 the relative errors $3.2\times10^{-4}$ and
$2.2\times10^{-3}$ are reported (the front of the Pe = 100 case is
under-resolved on this mesh, in both codes; no stabilisation in either).

$-\kappa\Delta u + \bm c\cdot\grad u + s u = f$ with $\kappa = 0.1$, $s = 1$,

\subsection{Nonlinear diffusion: the Kirchhoff case}
\file{nonlinear\_diffusion\_kirchhoff.yaml} (\file{nonlinear\_convection\_diffusion\_1D.cpp},
which has no convection despite its name): $m(u)\,\partial_t u - \dive(a(u)\grad u) = 0$

$\Delta t = 0.1$ to $t = 1$, full Newton to $10^{-8}$ relative.  With
$m_1/m_0 = a_1/a_0$ the Kirchhoff transform $\phi = a_0(u - u_{\mathrm{ref}}) + \tfrac12 a_1(u - u_{\mathrm{ref}})^2$
satisfies the linear heat equation, whose 1000-term series
(\file{nonlinear\_heat.m}: $\alpha = a_0/m_0$, $\kappa_1 = 10$, $\kappa_2 = 100$,

series is not an expression of the input).  Check: with the series as the
initial condition (the driver's, $300$ up to its truncation) and the rule
of order $2p + 2$, the error history against the driver's to $10^{-5}$

\section{Closed-form homogeneous states of incompressible models}
\label{app:homogeneous}
%============================================================================

The reference solutions of Section~\ref{sec:homogeneous} and of
\file{tests/test\_homogeneous} are collected here \cite{ogden}.  The models
themselves ($\Psi$, its derivatives and small-strain shear modulus for the
neo-Hookean, Mooney--Rivlin, Yeoh, Gent, Arruda--Boyce and Ogden energies)
are given in the theory manual.

\subsection{Stress of an incompressible isotropic solid}

With $J = \lambda_1\lambda_2\lambda_3 = 1$ the second invariant is
$\Itwo = \lambda_1^{-2} + \lambda_2^{-2} + \lambda_3^{-2} = \tr\bB^{-1}$ and
Cayley--Hamilton gives $\bB^2 = \Ione\bB - \Itwo\bI + \bB^{-1}$.  For
$\Psi = \Psi(\Ione, \Itwo)$ write $\Pone = \partial\Psi/\partial\Ione$,
$\Ptwo = \partial\Psi/\partial\Itwo$.  The constraint is enforced by a
multiplier that does no work and is fixed by equilibrium and the boundary
conditions, not by the constitutive law; only the isotropic part of the
stress is affected, so the convention-free statement is the
deviatoric--mean split
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;
  \bsig = \bsig' + p\,\bI, \qquad
  \bsig' = 2\Pone\,\dev\bB - 2\Ptwo\,\dev\bB^{-1}, \qquad
  p = \tfrac13\tr\bsig .\;}
  \label{eq:appsplit}
\end{equation}
Much of the literature writes
$\bsig = -\tilde p\,\bI + 2\Pone\bB - 2\Ptwo\bB^{-1}$ and calls $\tilde p$
the hydrostatic pressure; because authors absorb different isotropic terms
into $\tilde p$ its value depends on the convention, whereas
$p = \tfrac13\tr\bsig$ (tensile positive) does not, and it is the unknown
of the mixed formulation and the \code{pressure} output.  A compressive
hydrostatic state has $p < 0$.  In the principal frame
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_i = 2\Pone\lambda_i^2 - 2\Ptwo\lambda_i^{-2} - q
           = \lambda_i\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial\lambda_i} - q', \qquad
  \sigma_i - \sigma_j
  = 2\Pone(\lambda_i^2 - \lambda_j^2) - 2\Ptwo(\lambda_i^{-2} - \lambda_j^{-2})
  = \lambda_i\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial\lambda_i}
  - \lambda_j\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial\lambda_j},
  \label{eq:appdiff}
\end{equation}
so the principal stress differences are constitutive, and the recipe for
every homogeneous problem is the same: the two differences follow from
\eqref{eq:appdiff}, one principal stress is fixed by a traction-free face,
and then $p = \tfrac13(\sigma_1 + \sigma_2 + \sigma_3)$.  With $J = 1$ the
Piola--Kirchhoff stresses are $\bP = \bsig\bF^{-T}$ and
$\bS = \bF^{-1}\bsig\bF^{-T}$; for $\bF = \diag(\lambda_i)$,
$P_i = \sigma_i/\lambda_i$ (the nominal stress that a traction condition
of the total Lagrangian form prescribes) and $S_i = \sigma_i/\lambda_i^2$.
For small strains $\bsig \approx 2\mu\,\beps + p\,\bI$ with
$\mu = 2(\Pone + \Ptwo)|_{\Ione = \Itwo = 3}$, $E = 3\mu$, $\nu = \tfrac12$.

\paragraph{Models in $\Ione$ alone.}
Yeoh, Gent and Arruda--Boyce have $\Ptwo = 0$, so \eqref{eq:appsplit}
reduces to the neo-Hookean form with a deformation-dependent modulus,
$\bsig = \mu_{\mathrm{eff}}(\Ione)\dev\bB + p\bI$,
$\mu_{\mathrm{eff}} = 2\Pone(\Ione)$: every neo-Hookean result below
applies to them with $\mu$ replaced by $\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}$ at the $\Ione$ of
that deformation.  Ogden's model is not an invariant model and is
evaluated in the principal frame with \eqref{eq:appdiff},
$\lambda_i\partial\Psi/\partial\lambda_i = \sum_r\mu_r\lambda_i^{\alpha_r}$.

\subsection{The four modes}

A homogeneous deformation has constant $\bF$, hence constant $\bsig$ and
$\bP$; $\Dive\bP = 0$ holds identically, so the state is an exact solution
without body force and $p$ is a constant fixed by the traction-free faces.
Extension is $\lambda > 1$, compression $\lambda < 1$; the formulas cover
both.  NH is neo-Hookean, MR Mooney--Rivlin with $\mu = 2(c_1 + c_2)$.

\paragraph{Uniaxial tension and compression.}
$\bF = \diag(\lambda, \lambda^{-1/2}, \lambda^{-1/2})$,
$\Ione = \lambda^2 + 2/\lambda$, $\Itwo = 2\lambda + 1/\lambda^2$,
$\sigma_2 = \sigma_3 = 0$:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;
  \sigma_1 = 2\Bigl(\lambda^2 - \frac1\lambda\Bigr)\Bigl(\Pone + \frac{\Ptwo}{\lambda}\Bigr),
  \qquad
  P_1 = \frac{\sigma_1}{\lambda},
  \qquad
  p = \frac{\sigma_1}{3}.\;}
\end{equation}
\begin{align}
  \text{NH:}\quad & \sigma_1 = \mu\Bigl(\lambda^2 - \frac1\lambda\Bigr), &
  P_1 &= \mu\Bigl(\lambda - \frac1{\lambda^2}\Bigr), \\
  \text{MR:}\quad & \sigma_1 = 2\Bigl(c_1 + \frac{c_2}{\lambda}\Bigr)\Bigl(\lambda^2 - \frac1\lambda\Bigr), &
  P_1 &= 2\Bigl(c_1 + \frac{c_2}{\lambda}\Bigr)\Bigl(\lambda - \frac1{\lambda^2}\Bigr), \\
  \text{Ogden:}\quad & \sigma_1 = \sum_r\mu_r\bigl(\lambda^{\alpha_r} - \lambda^{-\alpha_r/2}\bigr), &
  P_1 &= \sum_r\mu_r\bigl(\lambda^{\alpha_r-1} - \lambda^{-\alpha_r/2-1}\bigr).
\end{align}
Small strain: $\sigma_1 \approx 3\mu(\lambda - 1) = E(\lambda - 1)$.

\paragraph{Equibiaxial tension.}
$\bF = \diag(\lambda, \lambda, \lambda^{-2})$,
$\Ione = 2\lambda^2 + 1/\lambda^4$, $\Itwo = \lambda^4 + 2/\lambda^2$,
$\sigma_3 = 0$:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;
  \sigma_1 = \sigma_2 = 2\Bigl(\lambda^2 - \frac1{\lambda^4}\Bigr)\bigl(\Pone + \lambda^2\Ptwo\bigr),
  \qquad
  P_1 = P_2 = \frac{\sigma_1}{\lambda},
  \qquad
  p = \frac{2\sigma_1}{3}.\;}
\end{equation}
NH: $\sigma_1 = \mu(\lambda^2 - \lambda^{-4})$; MR:
$\sigma_1 = 2(c_1 + c_2\lambda^2)(\lambda^2 - \lambda^{-4})$; Ogden:
$\sigma_1 = \sum_r\mu_r(\lambda^{\alpha_r} - \lambda^{-2\alpha_r})$.
Equibiaxial tension to $\lambda$ is uniaxial compression to $\lambda^{-2}$
plus a hydrostatic stress, which is why the two tests are interchangeable
for identifying $\Psi$.

\paragraph{Pure shear (planar tension).}
$\bF = \diag(\lambda, 1, \lambda^{-1})$,
$\Ione = \Itwo = \lambda^2 + 1 + 1/\lambda^2$, $\sigma_3 = 0$:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;
  \begin{aligned}
  \sigma_1 &= 2\Bigl(\lambda^2 - \frac1{\lambda^2}\Bigr)\bigl(\Pone + \Ptwo\bigr), &
  \sigma_2 &= 2\Bigl(1 - \frac1{\lambda^2}\Bigr)\bigl(\Pone + \lambda^2\Ptwo\bigr), \\
  P_1 &= \frac{\sigma_1}{\lambda},\quad P_2 = \sigma_2, &
  p &= \frac{\sigma_1 + \sigma_2}{3}.
  \end{aligned}\;}
  \label{eq:apppureshear}
\end{equation}
\begin{align}
  \text{NH:}\quad & \sigma_1 = \mu\Bigl(\lambda^2 - \frac1{\lambda^2}\Bigr), &
  \sigma_2 &= \mu\Bigl(1 - \frac1{\lambda^2}\Bigr), \\
  \text{MR:}\quad & \sigma_1 = \mu\Bigl(\lambda^2 - \frac1{\lambda^2}\Bigr), &
  \sigma_2 &= 2\bigl(c_1 + c_2\lambda^2\bigr)\Bigl(1 - \frac1{\lambda^2}\Bigr), \\
  \text{Ogden:}\quad & \sigma_1 = \sum_r\mu_r\bigl(\lambda^{\alpha_r} - \lambda^{-\alpha_r}\bigr), &
  \sigma_2 &= \sum_r\mu_r\bigl(1 - \lambda^{-\alpha_r}\bigr).
\end{align}
The principal stretches $(\lambda, 1, \lambda^{-1})$ are those of a simple
shear of amount $\gamma = \lambda - \lambda^{-1}$: pure shear is simple shear
followed by a rotation.  For Mooney--Rivlin $\sigma_1$ depends on $\mu$
alone; the constrained-direction stress $\sigma_2$ is what distinguishes
$c_1$ from $c_2$.

\paragraph{Simple shear.}
$\bF = \bI + \gamma\,\be_1\otimes\be_2$,
\begin{equation}
  \bB = \begin{pmatrix} 1+\gamma^2 & \gamma & 0 \\ \gamma & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}, \qquad
  \bB^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -\gamma & 0 \\ -\gamma & 1+\gamma^2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}, \qquad
  \Ione = \Itwo = 3 + \gamma^2 .
\end{equation}
The principal stretches are
$\lambda = \tfrac12(\gamma + \sqrt{\gamma^2 + 4})$, $\lambda^{-1}$ and $1$,
with the in-plane principal directions at the angle $\theta$ to $\be_1$,
$\tan2\theta = 2/\gamma$.  The deviatoric stress \eqref{eq:appsplit} is
$\sigma'_{12} = 2(\Pone + \Ptwo)\gamma$,
$\sigma'_{11} = \tfrac23(2\Pone + \Ptwo)\gamma^2$,
$\sigma'_{22} = -\tfrac23(\Pone + 2\Ptwo)\gamma^2$,
$\sigma'_{33} = -\tfrac23(\Pone - \Ptwo)\gamma^2$, which gives the shear
stress and the normal-stress differences independently of $p$:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;
  \begin{aligned}
  \sigma_{12} &= 2(\Pone + \Ptwo)\,\gamma, &
  \sigma_{11} - \sigma_{22} &= \gamma\,\sigma_{12} = 2(\Pone + \Ptwo)\gamma^2, \\
  \sigma_{11} - \sigma_{33} &= 2\Pone\gamma^2, &
  \sigma_{22} - \sigma_{33} &= -2\Ptwo\gamma^2 .
  \end{aligned}\;}
  \label{eq:appsimpleshear}
\end{equation}
The secant shear modulus $\sigma_{12}/\gamma = 2(\Pone + \Ptwo)$ is constant
for NH and MR (a linear shear response) and depends on $\gamma$ through
$\Ione = 3 + \gamma^2$ for the models in $\Ione$ alone; the relation
$\sigma_{11} - \sigma_{22} = \gamma\sigma_{12}$ is universal.  The mean stress
depends on which face is traction free:
\begin{align}
  \sigma_{33} = 0\ \text{(thin sheet):}\quad &
  \sigma_{11} = 2\Pone\gamma^2, & \sigma_{22} &= -2\Ptwo\gamma^2, &
  p &= \tfrac23(\Pone - \Ptwo)\gamma^2, \\
  \sigma_{22} = 0\ \text{(free top face, plane strain):}\quad &
  \sigma_{11} = 2(\Pone + \Ptwo)\gamma^2, & \sigma_{33} &= 2\Ptwo\gamma^2, &
  p &= \tfrac23(\Pone + 2\Ptwo)\gamma^2 .
\end{align}
NH (either case): $\sigma_{12} = \mu\gamma$, $\sigma_{11} = \mu\gamma^2$,
$\sigma_{22} = \sigma_{33} = 0$, $p = \mu\gamma^2/3$.  MR with
$\sigma_{33} = 0$: $\sigma_{11} = 2c_1\gamma^2$, $\sigma_{22} = -2c_2\gamma^2$,
$p = \tfrac23(c_1 - c_2)\gamma^2$; with $\sigma_{22} = 0$:
$\sigma_{11} = \mu\gamma^2$, $\sigma_{33} = 2c_2\gamma^2$,
$p = \tfrac23(c_1 + 2c_2)\gamma^2$.  Ogden:
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_{12} = \frac{\sum_r\mu_r(\lambda^{\alpha_r} - \lambda^{-\alpha_r})}{\lambda + \lambda^{-1}}, \qquad
  \sigma_{11} - \sigma_{22} = \gamma\sigma_{12}, \qquad
  \sigma_{11} + \sigma_{22} - 2\sigma_{33} = \sum_r\mu_r\bigl(\lambda^{\alpha_r} + \lambda^{-\alpha_r} - 2\bigr).
\end{equation}
The nominal stress follows from $\bF^{-T} = \bI - \gamma\,\be_2\otimes\be_1$:
$P_{11} = \sigma_{11} - \gamma\sigma_{12}$, $P_{12} = \sigma_{12}$,
$P_{21} = \sigma_{12} - \gamma\sigma_{22}$, $P_{22} = \sigma_{22}$,
$P_{33} = \sigma_{33}$, so that for NH $P_{11} = P_{22} = 0$ and
$P_{12} = P_{21} = \mu\gamma$: the nominal tractions on the sheared faces are
purely tangential.  For any other model the lateral faces
$X_1 = \text{const}$ must carry the normal nominal traction $P_{11}$ for
simple shear to be an exact solution.

\paragraph{Consistency checks.}
All stresses vanish at $\lambda = 1$ or $\gamma = 0$.  The initial slopes

\subsection{Reproducing the states in two dimensions}
\label{app:twod}

In two dimensions the kernels build $\bF$ from the in-plane block with
$F_{33} = 1$ (plane strain) or the thickness stretch (plane stress).  An
incompressible plane-strain state has in-plane principal stretches
$(\lambda, \lambda^{-1})$: up to a rotation it is the pure shear
\eqref{eq:apppureshear} with $\be_3$ as the constrained direction, and the
reaction $\sigma_{33}$ enters both $p$ and the von Mises stress.

\paragraph{Plane-strain extension.}
Stretch $\lambda$ along $\be_1$ with the faces $X_2 = 0, H$ traction free.
Relabelling \eqref{eq:apppureshear} with
$(\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3) = (\lambda, \lambda^{-1}, 1)$,
\begin{gather}
  \bu = (\lambda - 1)X_1\,\be_1 + (\lambda^{-1} - 1)X_2\,\be_2, \\
  \sigma_{11} = 2(\Pone + \Ptwo)\Bigl(\lambda^2 - \frac1{\lambda^2}\Bigr), \qquad
  \sigma_{33} = 2\Bigl(1 - \frac1{\lambda^2}\Bigr)\bigl(\Pone + \lambda^2\Ptwo\bigr), \\
  p = \frac{\sigma_{11} + \sigma_{33}}{3}, \qquad
  P_{11} = \frac{\sigma_{11}}{\lambda}, \qquad
  \sigma_{vm} = \sqrt{\sigma_{11}^2 + \sigma_{33}^2 - \sigma_{11}\sigma_{33}} ;
\end{gather}
NH: $\sigma_{11} = \mu(\lambda^2 - \lambda^{-2})$,
$\sigma_{33} = \mu(1 - \lambda^{-2})$,
$p = \tfrac13\mu(\lambda^2 + 1 - 2\lambda^{-2})$; MR: the same $\sigma_{11}$
with $\sigma_{33} = 2(c_1 + c_2\lambda^2)(1 - \lambda^{-2})$.  In an input
the affine $\bu$ is prescribed on the two end faces and $X_2 = 0, H$ are
left free (or $\bu$ on $X_1 = 0$ and the nominal traction $P_{11}\be_1$ on
$X_1 = L$); with Taylor--Hood spaces the affine displacement and the
constant pressure lie in the discrete spaces, so the mixed formulation
reproduces them to solver tolerance.

\paragraph{Plane-strain simple shear.}
$\bu = \gamma X_2\,\be_1$.  With the displacement prescribed on the whole
boundary (the patch test of \file{tests/test\_mixed}) the incompressible
pressure is determined only up to a constant (Piola identity), so the
checkable quantities are the deviatoric stress and the relations
\eqref{eq:appsimpleshear}; at finite $\kappa$ the affine field is exactly
isochoric, $p = \kappa(J - 1) = 0$ and $\bsig = \bsig'$.  If only the top
and bottom faces are constrained and the lateral faces are left free,
simple shear is \emph{not} the exact solution (the lateral faces would have
to carry $P_{11}$ and $P_{21}$); even for NH, where $P_{11} = 0$, the free
faces perturb the state near the edges because $P_{21} = \mu\gamma \ne 0$.

\paragraph{Plane stress.}
For a thin sheet $\sigma_{i3} = 0$ and the thickness stretch is set by the
material.  For an incompressible material $\lambda_3 = 1/\det\bF_{2D}$ and
$\sigma_{33} = 0$ fixes the mean stress as $p = -\sigma'_{33}$,
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_{\alpha\beta} = \sigma'_{\alpha\beta} - \sigma'_{33}\,\delta_{\alpha\beta},
  \qquad
  \bP = \bP_{\mathrm{iso}} + p\,\bF^{-T}, \quad p = -\lambda_3 P_{\mathrm{iso},33},
\end{equation}
so the sheet is a displacement problem in the two in-plane components with
no pressure unknown, no inf-sup condition and no locking.  The four modes
above are all plane-stress states and become two-dimensional problems: the
uniaxial sheet with
$\bu = (\lambda - 1)X_1\be_1 + (\lambda^{-1/2} - 1)X_2\be_2$ on the end faces
and the lateral faces free, and equibiaxial tension, pure shear and
thin-sheet simple shear with the affine displacement on the whole boundary,
which is well posed here because $p$ is determined pointwise.  Since each
load step starts with the boundary displaced and the interior lagging,
shear-dominated sheet problems need increments that shear a boundary layer
of elements by no more than a few percent, as the energy grows like
$(\det\bF_{2D})^{-2}$.
